# User guide

The full tour of the planner: every screen of profile creation, the ways to make a plan your own, ADA, and exactly what the calculator does and does not model. The [README](../README.md) has the short version.

## Saves and profiles

Each **user → named save → profile** has independent progress. Trying another profile does not reset the original. Profiles store a calculation snapshot, so updating the software cannot silently change existing targets. Rename saves and profiles from **Saves & profiles**.

**Importing** a full export or a share under **Backup → Import saves** adds each save in the file as a new copy, named after the file and the time it was exported, for example _Coop world (from anna.json, exported 2026-10-04 14:05)_; a copy that would get a name you already have is numbered (_· 2_). The profile you have open stays open, so what you tick next never lands in someone else's copy: **Saves & profiles** then lists the copies with an **Open** button for each. Rename a copy as you like; its name is only a label.

**Before anything is imported, the planner tells you what is in the file.** After you choose a file under **Import saves**, a question lists each profile in it with its phase and how many of its build-plan steps are done (ticked, or done by their own condition, as on the profile card), for example _anna.json holds the save “Coop world” with 2 profiles, exported 2026-10-21 14:05_ with _Main: Phase 3, 412 of 980 steps done_, and says that importing adds a copy next to your profiles without replacing anything. On the Docker edition, **Backup → Restore this profile…** asks the same way before it replaces the open profile's progress with a progress backup: _This replaces the progress of “Main”: 412 of 980 steps done now, 380 in the file._ **Cancel** changes nothing. A damaged file, or one made by a newer version of the planner, is refused with a message saying why, and no question is asked.

**Each browser tab keeps the profile it opened.** A new tab opens the profile you (or anyone using your account, or the server without accounts) opened last; a tab that is already open, or that you reload, stays on its own profile, and everything you tick or write there is saved to the profile that tab shows. When someone opens another profile in another tab or on another device, for example with **Duplicate** or **Try another profile**, a tab still showing the old one says so at the top of the page: _Another tab or player moved to “Main · copy”_, with **Open “Main · copy”** to follow them and **Stay on “Main”** to keep working where you are.

**Compare profiles.** A save with two or more profiles shows **Compare profiles** under its cards: per phase, each profile's buildings, buildings to add, build materials, power needed, ore mined, Crude Oil, alternate recipes to unlock (the recipes the phase's hard-drive step asks for) and delivery time, each with its difference from the **Baseline** profile (− is fewer or sooner, + more or later). **Buildings to add** are the machines the phase adds to its production lines: all of them in the first phase, then only those beyond what the phase before runs on the same line, as the build plan's handover counts them; **Build materials** are the parts those buildings cost to build. Miners, extractors, belts and pipes are not counted. The baseline starts as the profile you have open; pick another one, or another **Phase**, to compare from there. Nothing is saved by these choices. **Time to finish** lists every planned phase's time and the total, and marks the **Fastest** profile among those that plan the same phases as the baseline. It is the phase's delivery time, as the build plan gives it (_Delivery in about …_), plus an estimated building time: the buildings to add times the **Minutes per building (estimate)** you set above the table (3 unless you change it). The planner does not measure how long building takes, so that part is your estimate, and each cell says how much of it is delivery and how much estimated building ("4 h 10 min delivery + est. 45 min building"). The minutes are remembered in this browser, not saved with a profile; set 0 to compare delivery time only. The other figures are the ones each profile's plan was calculated with; nothing is recalculated, and a figure an older plan does not hold shows "—".

## Create a profile

Open **Saves & profiles → Create a save**, or **Try another profile** on an existing save.

A profile you leave unnamed is named after what it is: its goal, what differs from the profile it starts from (or from a new save's defaults) and the day, for example _Minimal construction · exact ratios · Oct 7_. Profiles you already have keep their names: a card still named after its goal alone, such as _Balanced progression_, offers **Rename to “Balanced progression · exact ratios · Oct 7”**, with the day its plan was made (numbered _· 2_ when two cards would get the same name). Nothing changes until you press it; **✕** keeps the name, and the offer does not come back.

### Edit a profile's settings

**Edit settings** on a profile card, or in the profile menu at the foot of the sidebar, opens the settings that profile was calculated with. Change what you need; nothing is recalculated until you say so. Review then lists **what changes**: the settings you changed, and per phase the production lines (how many, how many are new or gone), the buildings and the power needed. **Recalculate in place** gives the profile the new plan and keeps your progress: unlocks, the storage room, notes and deliveries stay, and a production line you marked running stays ticked where the new plan needs no more machines and no more input; one that needs more machines keeps the ones you built and says how many to add; one that needs fewer stays ticked and says how many machines are spare; one the new plan makes with another recipe says what to switch ("Switch 4 Constructors from Screws to Cast Screws, and add 1"); one the new plan no longer has at all is listed over the steps as no longer needed, to dismantle or keep; and any other change is kept unticked for you to review. Because you go on in the same profile, it also stays on the phase you are working on, and every other step the new plan still lists keeps its tick: the Space Elevator, the mining, retirement and delivery steps, the Alien Power Augmenters and the hand-fed somersloops. A step the new plan no longer has loses its tick. The version before the edit is kept whole as a separate profile, _<name> (before edit, Oct 7, 2:05 PM)_, with all of its progress, so you can open it again at any time. Its card says _Kept version of “<name>”_, and its ⋯ menu has **Restore this version…**: after you confirm, the kept plan and progress go back into the edited profile, which keeps its name and place, and the version they replace is kept in turn as _<name> (before restore, Oct 7, 2:05 PM)_, so you can switch back the same way. Nothing is deleted. A version kept before this option existed, and a copy made with Duplicate, has no Restore; open it instead. The recalculation offers on the build plan, **Factories** and **Logistics** (exact clocks, somersloop pins, items made on site, transport fuel, what your ticks show you have) recalculate the open profile in place the same way: they ask first, name the backup they keep, and carry your progress as Edit settings does. The top bar names the open profile after its save, as the profile menu and the build plan do.

### The guided start

A new save opens on a short illustrated sequence rather than the full settings form. Six questions, each a picture and a sentence: which phase you are on, what the plan should do for you, which recipes it may use, what it should keep stocked, how exact the build should be, and how it should power your factory (coal, fuel or the planner's choice, with the spare power you already have). The questions speak about your start phase: the stock question quotes the extra buildings from that phase on, and a material your phase cannot make yet says from which phase it is stocked. Everything else keeps a default that was chosen by measuring what it actually changes — eleven of the settings form's controls, including the world seed, SAM conversion, somersloops held and every resource budget, do not move a default plan at all.

Some of the questions are about your world rather than about the plan. On Phase 1 you are asked whether the HUB tutorial is behind you; if it is, its steps start ticked.

From Phase 2 on the next screen asks **what you already have**, and all of it is optional: **Everything before Phase N is done**, the miners and belts you already have where they are better than your start phase's own, the generators you already have, and the alternates you already own. These are the choices Review and steps 2 and 4 of All settings offer (see **What you already have** below), and Review shows them the same way. Skip the screen and nothing changes: the earlier steps stay on the build plan to tick, and each phase plans with its own miners and belts.

From Phase 2 on you are asked **what you are already producing**, as a rate: search for the item, then type what it makes per minute. Leave the rate blank and that line simply is not counted yet — the row stays where it is and says so. The planner credits that and builds only the remainder — and it does not build the chain behind it either, so the iron, the plastic and the power that line consumes all leave the plan with it. What you built it out of is your business: the recipe and the machine count do not have to match anything this plan would have chosen, which is the whole point of entering a rate rather than ticking the planner's own list. Declaring 50/min of something a phase only needs 15/min of simply credits the 15; the rest is yours. Nothing is pre-ticked, because there is no build step to tick — the line is not in the plan at all. The same control is in **All settings → Game settings**, beside spare existing power.

One assumption comes with it, the same one that already applies to spare power: an existing line's ore and electricity are already spent in your world, so enter your resource budgets and spare power net of it. Review states this every time it credits something, and names what it credited.

**All settings →** is on every screen and opens the five-step wizard below at the step that owns the same question, keeping every answer; **← Guided start** comes back the same way. Picking _I will choose them myself_ for recipes shows the recipe list on the same screen, and the questions go on after it. Picking _as fast as the map allows_ for the goal adds one screen after it: your resource budgets, the boxes of the Resources step, which you can change there or work out from your nodes. Maximum output needs them confirmed, so **Continue** waits for **I have checked these budgets for my save**; then the questions go on as for any other goal.

The last step is **Review**: the calculated plan, phase by phase, with **Create profile**. **Back** returns to the questions, and **All settings →** shows the same Review in the five-step wizard, where every setting is a tab away.

Adding a profile to a save you already play starts from that save's settings instead, and asks only **what is different this time?**

### All settings

1. Name the save and enter its phase, purity/distribution, elevator multiplier, consumption multiplier, recipe part cost multiplier and spare existing power. The recipe part cost is the Game Modes setting (0.25× to 2×, 1× by default): every recipe takes that many times its usual inputs for the same outputs (the Packager's packing and unpacking keep their usual inputs, so packing a fluid and unpacking it never makes more of it), so the plan's lines, raw resources, power and feasibility all follow it. A plan keeps the multiplier it was calculated with: the sidebar and the profile's card on Saves & profiles name it when it is not 1×, and only Edit settings, with Review naming the change, recalculates it. Under it, **Count what buildings cost to build** (off by default) makes every phase also make the parts its production lines, generators, miners and extractors cost to build (the building costs of the build plan's steps), within the phase's time and on its budgets, as well as its deliveries. Each phase counts all of its own buildings, since a phase plan is complete in itself, except the machines of lines you already built and the generators you already have; a part no recipe of the phase can make, such as the Portable Miners a Miner takes (made by hand in the Equipment Workshop), is left out. A phase that can make them keeps exactly the plan it has without the box; one that cannot is a draft whose reason names the building that costs the most to build and its parts ("Counting what its buildings cost to build, this phase does not fit in its 1 h: its 154 Miners Mk.3 need 7,700 × Steel Pipe, … to build, and its 8,761 other buildings need parts too, which it can't make in time along with its deliveries."), with what runs out, the hours it fits in and the option to stop counting them. It is checked at exact clocks without production amplification, as drafts are, and needs a time target: maximum output does not count it. The sidebar and the profile card say "Build costs counted" on such a plan.

   **Caps per phase**, under it, set the most a phase may build and draw: **At most this many buildings per phase** and **At most this many MW per phase**, whole numbers, blank for no cap (the default). Buildings count every machine and generator on the production lines, as the plan’s building count does, the last underclocked machine of a line included; miners and extractors are not lines. MW is the power the phase needs: its lines with the utility allowance, and its miners and extractors. A phase whose plan already keeps within the caps is exactly the plan it would be without them. A phase over a cap is planned again within it by its other means (other alternates, clocks, more buildings for less power or the other way round), and storage-only lines that would take it over a cap are left out, so its containers take only the surplus. A phase that cannot keep within a cap becomes a draft whose reason names the cap and what the phase needs: “at least” where that is proven (“This phase needs at least 102 buildings; the cap is 50 buildings.”), otherwise what its plan without the cap needs (“Without the cap, this phase's plan needs 920.5 MW; the cap is 700 MW.”), never more. It says when the search for a plan within the cap stopped at its time limit, offers turning off whole machines when the plan at exact clocks keeps within the caps, and otherwise the time per phase from which it may fit. A plan keeps the caps it was calculated with: the sidebar names them, and only Edit settings, with Review naming the change (“Caps per phase”), recalculates it. **Best belt the plan may use**, beside them, caps the belt tier: Mk.2 to Mk.5 belts, or **No cap** (the default). No phase then plans with a better belt, even where its milestones unlock one or you already have one (the cap wins over **Belts you already have**). With **Each phase's budgets follow the miners and belts it can build** ticked, a node gives no more than the capped belt carries, so a capped phase taps more nodes and its budgets, node advice and mining step follow; the plan’s note on what the budgets follow names the belts each phase uses. The belt advice on Logistics and in a production line's dialog never suggests a better belt, for every plan. A cap at or above every phase's belt changes nothing. A phase that runs short because of the cap becomes the usual draft, whose options include raising or clearing the belt cap. A plan keeps its belt cap until you change it in Edit settings (Review names it as “Best belt the plan may use”). **At most this many hard-drive alternates in the whole plan**, last, caps the hard drives: a whole number from 0 (none beyond the ones you own), blank for no cap (the default). It counts the alternate recipes the plan's hard-drive steps ask for over all its phases together, since a recipe you unlock stays unlocked; **Alternates you already own** and the recipes a MAM node or a milestone unlocks (Turbofuel, Compacted Coal, Polyester Fabric, Distilled Silica) do not count. A plan that already counts on no more is exactly the plan it would be without the cap. Otherwise the planner picks the set of alternates that serves the plan's last phase best within the cap (a search of up to 15 seconds) and plans every phase from that set, so an earlier phase may do without an alternate it would have liked; the plan's notes name the set, and the hard-drive steps list only those recipes. A phase that does not fit its budgets with the set becomes the usual draft, whose reason names the cap and whose options include raising or clearing it; when the search stopped at its time limit, the plan keeps the alternates the plan without the cap uses most and says so. A plan keeps its cap until you change it in Edit settings (Review names it as “Cap on hard drives”). **Best next hard drive** on the build plan ranks the alternates as if there were no cap.
2. Choose recipe access (standard, all alternates, or hand-pick specific hard-drive alternates: each recipe is **Owned**, **Hunting** or **Not allowed**; with nothing hunting or owned the plan is the same as with standard recipes), whether to use the MAM recipes Turbofuel, Compacted Coal and Polyester Fabric (on by default, whatever the recipe access; a turbofuel power route keeps Turbofuel and Compacted Coal), pure ingots, SAM conversion policy, nuclear recycling, protected storage and extra Singularity Cell production.

   When you hand-pick alternates, the picker groups them by product, with a line saying what the alternate skips (for example "Cast Screws skips Iron Rod"). **Owned** recipes are ones you have unlocked: the plan uses them freely and their unlock steps start ticked. **Hunting** recipes are allowed in the plan, and their hard-drive steps stay on the build plan’s hunt until you mark them unlocked; each row names the standard recipe the product is made with until you find it. **Not allowed** recipes are never used. Filter by name, phase or state. In Edit settings, after you rank the alternates on the build plan, each row also says how many buildings it saves.

   In the build plan's **Hard drives** tab, **Best next hard drive** ranks on request what each alternate would change from the phase on screen to the end of the plan, and what each recipe you are hunting saves once found. A ranking can take up to 2 minutes, or longer while other rankings are running; one that reaches that limit shows the alternates it checked and says so. It sorts by buildings saved, can hide the alternates that save fewer than a threshold, and **Add as hunting** recalculates the profile in place with that recipe allowed, after asking, keeping the current version as a backup.

   Protected storage takes the plan's surplus first and adds optional storage-only lines only for items with no surplus (see **Calculation scope**); a plan calculated before that reserved production for every selected item, permanently, so it was often the largest single cost in a plan. There, on the default Phase 3/4/5 plan, raising **Construction materials refill /min** from 1 to 5 added 81% more buildings, and raising it to 20 added 425%, because it applies to all eighteen construction materials at once. A per-item rate is far cheaper for the same reassurance: storage takes the surplus first and a storage-only line tops up the rest, so 20/min of one item costs 1 to 4 more buildings on a default plan, because the plan already spills far more than that into storage as surplus — a default Phase 3 plan sends 29 Wire and 19 Iron Plate a minute to storage on top of the reserved rate, and 2 Concrete. That is why the guided start leaves the general rates at 1 and offers per-item floors instead. It is set by two rates: **Construction materials refill /min** for the parts you carry out by hand (plates, rods, concrete, wire, cable, beams, pipes, frames, plastic, rubber) and **Other items refill /min** for everything else. **Per-item storage rates** overrides single items; `0` keeps an item's container and address without reserving any production for it, and surplus still fills it. Settings saved before this existed use their one rate for both, so existing profiles are unchanged.

   Space Elevator parts start at `0`. Deliveries and the later project parts that consume them are already planned, so a standing buffer on top would be production nobody draws from. They keep their containers and addresses; enter a rate against one if you want a buffer anyway.

   **Somersloops.** Record how many somersloops you have to spend, how many Alien Power Augmenters Phase 5 builds and how many of those you will fuel. Each augmenter costs 10 somersloops permanently, generates 500 MW and multiplies the whole grid's base production — 10% unfueled, 30% fueled — so step 1 also asks for your **total installed power**, not only the spare part of it. Fuel is derived, never entered: each fueled augmenter adds 5 Alien Power Matrix/min to the plan and the Quantum Encoder chain behind it. Review then compares that plan against the same plan unfueled, like with like: the power the fuel adds to Phase 5's grid (30% instead of 10% on its base production) against the demand its fuel line adds, with the net gain or loss, and beside it the buildings of both plans (or their delivery times for maximum output). Power the plan does not need can still cost buildings, and Review says so.

   Somersloops parked in hand-fed constructors — power slugs into Power Shards, creature remains into Alien DNA, biomass into Solid Biofuel — reserve a somersloop and add a checklist step, in Phase 3 or your start phase if that is later: machines take somersloops only after the MAM research Production Amplifier; the build plan lists that research too. Their inputs are carried in by hand, so they stay out of the continuous production balance.

   The **somersloop ledger** under these boxes counts what you commit as you type: 10 per augmenter, 1 per hand-fed line and the amplification budget, against the somersloops you have. Your somersloops are a hard limit: a plan that commits more than you have is not calculated, and the ledger and Review say what to change. A profile calculated before this limit keeps its plan and numbers; its pages warn that it commits more somersloops than you have, and Edit settings takes you to the ledger.

   **Production amplification** is optional and starts at 0. Give it a budget and the planner puts that many somersloops in the plan's own machines, choosing where they pay best: an amplified machine keeps its inputs, doubles its output and draws four times the power, so it buys ore and buildings with power. Amplification starts in Phase 3: machines take somersloops only after the MAM research Production Amplifier, which needs SAM Fluctuators and Circuit Boards, so Phases 1 and 2 are planned without it and the build plan lists the research's step in Phase 3. A plan calculated before amplification waited for that research keeps its Phase 1-2 amplified lines and says so on its plan, factories and resources pages until you recalculate it. Collecting every somersloop is a hunt, and some are hard to reach, so leaving this at 0 plans exactly as before. The budget applies to each phase's plan rather than adding up across phases, because each phase is a self-contained steady state and the somersloops move as you rebuild.

   **Where a somersloop pays off most**, in the build plan's **Somersloops** tab from Phase 3 on, lists what one amplified machine would save on each line of the phase that could take somersloops, at the plan's own rates: "1 somersloop on Iron Plate: −1 Constructor, −30 Iron Ore/min, +1 MW". The machines are that line's, the raw resources and the power include everything upstream of it, and the lines are ranked by raw resources saved per somersloop (Water does not count), so a Manufacturer's four somersloops are weighed against four Constructors' one each. Every line is listed; a line's name opens its production line dialog. It is an estimate and recalculates nothing: to plan with somersloops, raise the amplification budget here. It is hidden in New to Satisfactory mode.

   **Somersloop pins.** To decide yourself how many somersloops a production line gets, open the line (from the build plan, a Factories card or the Somersloops ranking) and use **Somersloops on this line**, from Phase 3 on, when the plan has an amplification budget. Type a number and press **Pin**: whole machines only, so a Constructor takes any number and a Manufacturer a multiple of 4. A pin of 0 keeps somersloops off that line. **Planner’s choice** removes the pin. Nothing is recalculated when you pin: the build plan and Factories page say the plan needs a recalculation, and **Recalculate in place with somersloop pins** plans it, keeping the current version as a backup. Edit settings plans your pins too, and Review lists them. The plan then keeps every pin exactly and places the rest of each phase's budget itself. A pin it cannot keep is never changed silently: the phase is planned without it, and the plan's warnings, ADA and the line's dialog say why (the pins of a phase add up to more than the amplification budget, the recipe is not available in that phase, or no plan of the phase fits with it) and what to change. Power Shard pins for production lines come later.

3. Choose minimal construction (the fewest buildings that deliver within 24 hours, run as fast as they allow), balanced (8-hour delivery), a target time, or maximum elevator output. The whole-machine box shows what whole machines cost on your settings once the live estimate has measured it.

   **What the plan keeps lowest** decides what the plan saves on while it meets the goal: **Fewest buildings** (the default), **Least power** (the power its production lines, miners and extractors draw), **Least raw resources** (the ore, oil and gas it mines per minute; Water does not count, since it never runs out) or a **Weighted mix**, three whole weights from 0 to 10 for buildings, power and raw resources, where one building counts as much as 10 MW or 10 raw resources per minute, each times its weight. Least power and least raw resources usually build more machines to save it, and fewer buildings break a tie. Under Minimal construction the 24-hour plan keeps the objective too. A plan keeps the objective it was calculated with: the sidebar, the profile's card on Saves & profiles and ADA name it when it is not the fewest buildings, and only Edit settings, with Review naming the change, recalculates it.

   **Target time applies to** decides whether that time is each phase's target or only the final phase's. On _The final phase_, earlier phases run their lines as hard as the machines a later phase already builds allow, so they finish sooner without adding a building the plan later drops — an early phase is never made slower, never runs more of a recipe than a later phase keeps, and never uses a recipe no later phase uses. Their delivery rates are not rounded, and Review shows what each pulled-forward phase would otherwise have taken. Ignored for maximum output, which already maximizes every phase.

4. Review/edit available raw resource budgets. Confirm these before maximum-output planning.

   **Work these out from my nodes** opens a short survey instead, because a rate per minute is not something anyone knows off the top of their head. It asks what you can actually read off a map: which miner you will use and at what clock, how many impure, normal and pure nodes your world holds for each ore, and how many resource-well satellites you have for crude oil and nitrogen. It then does the arithmetic — a normal node is 60/min on a Mk.1, 120 on a Mk.2 and 240 on a Mk.3, halved when impure and doubled when pure, all multiplied by the clock; an oil node is 120/min normal and a well satellite 60/min, neither of which has miner marks.

   **You usually do not have to count anything.** The counting screens carry the game's own two **World Randomization** settings — _Resource node randomization_ and _Resource node purity_, with the same options in the same order — and fill the counts in from them. Set them to what you chose when you created the save and the whole world appears; change either and the counts follow. They are the same two settings as on **Game settings**, so if you set the purity there the survey opens already filled in.

   That works because the purity settings do not move nodes or add any: they shift every node up or down the purity scale. Each setting is therefore the known map count rearranged, and lands on exactly the budget the planner already ships for it — All Pure gives 127 pure iron nodes and 152,400 iron/min, the same number as the planner's own all-pure table. Anything you counted yourself that the table does not cover, such as oil well satellites, is left alone.

   **Random** randomization is a shuffle: it moves which resource sits at each location, but every resource keeps its node count — nineteen SAM nodes are still nineteen SAM nodes, somewhere else. So under Random the counts still fill in for the uniform purities (All Pure, Average, All Impure), where the split across purities stops mattering. Random shuffles the purities too, so for Default, Mostly Pure and Mostly Impure the totals are still right but the split is yours to count, and the survey says exactly that rather than refusing outright.

   **Reset all counts to zero** is on both counting screens, for when you would rather enter your own map than correct a filled-in one. It clears every ore, well, left-out and committed amount, and your miner mark and clock speed stay because those are equipment rather than counts. Nothing is asked first — **Undo reset** takes its place in the same spot and puts back exactly what was on screen, until you fill the counts in again or leave the survey.

   **Resource wells are the exception.** A well is randomized as a whole rather than satellite by satellite, and the map's seventeen wells carry different numbers of satellites, so a shuffle that leaves every ordinary node count untouched can still land nitrogen on a bigger or smaller well than it had. Under Random the survey fills the ordinary nodes and leaves the nitrogen wells for you to count, and says why.

   The **resource-rich** distributions genuinely do change how many nodes each resource has, and players who have generated them get answers a third apart from one seed to the next, so no table can be right — those are not filled in, and neither is a Random purity. What the survey does say is which way each one pushes the map, because every count published so far agrees on that much: Basic favours limestone, iron, copper and coal, Advanced favours caterium, sulfur, bauxite, quartz, uranium and SAM, and Fossil Fuel favours coal, oil and sulfur. If counts from another world are still on screen the survey says so rather than passing them off as yours.

   For a seed like that — or any world you have edited — the survey links to the [Satisfactory Calculator interactive map](https://satisfactory-calculator.com/en/interactive-map), where uploading your save lists every node your world holds by purity. That upload is between you and that site; this planner never sends your save anywhere, and the map also works without one.

   The last screen takes off whatever extraction is **already committed** to factories this plan does not include, which is what makes a budget mean _free for this plan to use_. That is a different question from **what you are already producing** on the guided start: this one takes raw ore off the top, that one credits finished parts. Water is deliberately not counted — extractors sit on any lake or ocean, so the planner keeps its standing allowance, editable like any other budget. Your answers are stored with the profile, so the survey reopens where you left it.

   **Leave nodes out of this plan**, folded under the counts on both counting screens, is for nodes the plan must not use: one far from where you build, or one already feeding a factory the plan does not include. Enter how many of each purity to leave out, at most the nodes counted beside it; the ore screen asks for ores, the oil screen for crude oil nodes and well satellites. The plan never taps a node you leave out: the last screen takes them off the pool (a **Left out** column, before what is already committed), each phase's budget and the build plan's **Tap the resource nodes** use only the others, and so does the step that upgrades the tapped nodes before tapping more. The plan never draws more of a resource than its other nodes give, whatever budget you enter for it. Leave out every node of a resource and the plan cannot mine it: the last screen names it, and a phase that needs it does not fit, its draft saying that your node survey leaves every node of it out. A plan keeps the nodes it was calculated with: the sidebar, its card on **Saves & profiles** and ADA say how many it leaves out, and only **Edit settings**, through the survey and **Recalculate in place**, changes them (Review names the change).

   **Each phase's budgets follow the miners and belts it can build** is ticked for a new plan. The budgets you enter are then the most any phase draws, and each phase gets what its own equipment takes from the same nodes, as the game unlocks it: Miner Mk.1 in Phase 1, Mk.2 (Tier 4) from Phase 2 and Mk.3 (Tier 8) from Phase 4; Mk.2 belts in Phase 1 up to Mk.6 belts (Tier 9) in Phase 5, and Mk.2 pipes from Phase 3; Oil Extractors from Phase 3 and resource wells from Phase 4. Miners and extractors run at 100% in Phase 1, before the MAM is usable, and at up to 250% from Phase 2: overclocking takes Power Shards, made from the Power Slugs you collect (MAM: Blue Power Slugs and Overclock Production), and the build plan has that research before the first nodes it taps overclocked (in a profile made for Phase 2 or later, **Research Power Shards before tapping the nodes**, right before **Tap the resource nodes**). A node never gives more than its belt or pipe carries, so each node is planned at its belt cap: in Phase 3 a pure node's Miner Mk.2 fills its Mk.4 belt (480/min) at 200%, so 2,356.75 Iron Ore a minute takes 5 miners instead of 10, and in Phase 4 a pure node's Mk.3 miner fills its Mk.5 belt (780/min) at 162.5%. When a later phase needs more ore than the nodes already tapped give, the build plan upgrades those nodes first: **Upgrade the belts to Mk.5 and the miners to Mk.3 before tapping more nodes** (or the pipes, or the clock) comes right before that phase's **Tap the resource nodes**, names each milestone and gives, per resource, what the tapped nodes give before and after and how many new nodes are left ("3 new nodes, 8 without the upgrade"); the mining table then says which of its nodes are new ("8 pure (5 tapped in Phase 3, 3 new)"). To plan every phase at 100% instead, set a node survey's clock speed to 100%. On the default map that is 10% of the endgame budgets in Phase 1, about 31–47% in Phase 2, 44% and more in Phase 3 (all of the crude oil), about 78–88% in Phase 4 (79% for Iron Ore; all of the crude oil, nitrogen and uranium) and all of them in Phase 5; the table under the box gives the shares for your settings. A node survey's miner and clock cap every phase's, so a survey at Mk.2 and 150% never plans a Mk.3. If you already run better miners than a phase would unlock, choose them under **Miners you already have**: Miner Mk.3 there plans Phase 3 with Mk.3 too (at the phase's own clock), and the table follows the choice; a survey still caps it. Likewise **Belts you already have**: Mk.4 belts there carry a node's full 480/min under a Miner Mk.3 in Phase 2, where the phase's own Mk.3 belts carry 270/min, and the belt advice on Logistics and in a production line's **Belts & pipes** plans with your Mk.4 belts too. Untick it and every phase plans with the budgets as entered, as profiles made before it do. A new profile for a save you already play keeps the choice of the profile it starts from.

   Under the budgets, **Generators you already have** takes a count of each kind your start phase has unlocked: Biomass Burners, Coal Generators (from Phase 2), Fuel Generators (from Phase 3) and Nuclear Power Plants (from Phase 4). Each phase counts them among the generators it runs where it plans that kind, so you build only the rest: with 4 Coal Generators, a Phase 2 plan that runs 6 asks you to build 2 more, and with 8 it builds none and has 150 MW more headroom. They still burn fuel from your budgets, because a generator burns fuel only for the power drawn and the plan plans that fuel; a phase that plans none of that kind (Phase 3 burning fuel instead of coal) does not count them, and its **Power available now** says how to keep them running as spare power instead. Biomass Burners shorten the biomass burner bank. Leave a field empty for none.

5. Review calculated buildings, delivery time, power and feasibility by phase, then create the profile.

   **Power needed** holds the phase's production lines, its miners and extractors, and an allowance for trains, drone ports and pumps: **Extra utilities power** on the Preferences step, 20% of the production lines by default. Review and its live estimate, the build plan's "Power available now" step and the Power & resources page say how much of the figure that allowance is ("624.99 GW, incl. 96.62 GW for trains, drones and pumps (20%)"); Review's note and the Power & resources page lead to the setting; the latter opens **Edit settings** of the open profile on the Preferences step, so **Recalculate in place** applies it to this profile and keeps the version before as a backup. Particle Accelerators, Converters and Quantum Encoders count at their peak, and the pages say so: they draw less on average, so your real margin is larger than the figures show.

### Continuing a save you already play

When you add a profile to a save you are already playing, Review also offers **Continue the progress in this save**. A new plan does not undo the world: choose the profile to carry from and which of its records come along — milestone/MAM and hard-drive unlocks, the storage room layout and its built containers, power and start-up steps, elevator deliveries already handed in, notes and personal tasks, build-plan edits and factories, and progress on production lines. A line the new plan does not expand stays ticked. A line that now needs more machines keeps the machines you built: its step is unticked, says "5 of 6 built" under its title and opens with a table of the machines and inputs built, needed and to add ("5 Refineries built, so add 1, which takes 30 m³/min more Heavy Oil Residue"); tick it once all of them stand. If a later plan needs no more machines than you built on it, it comes out ticked again. A line that now needs fewer machines stays ticked and says "6 built, 1 spare" under its title: switch the spare ones off, dismantle them, or underclock the line; it never asks you to build them again. A line the new plan makes with another recipe (an alternate, or the standard recipe again) hands its machines to the new line, whose step says what to switch, how many to add, and which machines are spare or, on another kind of machine, can be dismantled or kept; tick it once the switch is done. A line the new plan has no use for at all is listed over the steps as **No longer needed**, with its machines, to dismantle or keep; it is never dropped silently. Any other change (more input on the same machines) is carried unticked so you can review it, as **Round up production** does. Hand-picking alternate recipes for a save you play also states which recipes you own, so their unlock steps can start ticked. The recipes **Planner's choice** ticked are not counted: they stay on the hard-drive hunt until you mark them unlocked. A recipe you untick and tick again in the picker counts as one you picked yourself. Nothing is moved: the profile you carry from keeps all of it, and a brand new save still starts empty.


When a recalculation carries progress on production lines (Recalculate in place, or a new profile that continues another one), the production lines you ticked as built, or partly built, count as machines that already stand. The new plan keeps and extends them instead of replacing them: it switches a built line to another recipe, or moves its somersloops, only when that saves more new machines than the switch needs. The same holds under every choice of what the plan keeps lowest: under least power or least raw resources, the power and ore a built line already uses count as spent, so the plan does not switch it to save a little power or ore. Under maximum output a built line gives way only to speed: the plan keeps it unless switching makes the phase more than 1% faster, and then switches only as many machines as that needs. A built machine the new plan does not need is never run just for what it makes on the side: it stays idle, and its step says it is spare. Under the fewest buildings, and under a weighted mix that gives buildings a weight, a built line may also run more machines than a plan with nothing built would give it, to save new machines elsewhere, but only while no raw resource the phase mines rises by more than 5% and nothing more goes to the AWESOME Sink. So 9 Fuel Refineries built where the plan needs 3 run 4, not all 9 at nearly 30% more Crude Oil; the other 5 are spare. Review's **What changes** says how many built production lines are kept.
### What you already have

For a game already under way, Review also has **What you already have**. **Everything before Phase N is done** ticks the HUB milestone and MAM research steps that the phases before your start phase list, so the new profile opens on Phase N instead of on a list of earlier steps; while such an earlier phase still has an open step, its build plan offers **Mark everything before Phase N done** to do the same later. **Alternates you own** lists each alternate recipe the plan's hard-drive steps ask for: tick the ones you already have and their unlock steps start ticked, and a phase's hard-drive hunt too once you own every recipe it asks for, so you only hunt for the rest. Both only tick steps, and you can untick any of them; records carried from another profile come first. Everything starts unticked. The guided start asks for the same choices on one screen right after your phase. Step 2 (How do you want to build?) has **Alternates you already own** for the recipes you have unlocked before: they can join the plan even with Standard recipes, from the phase each becomes available, and Review shows the ones the plan uses as owned, so their unlock steps start ticked and no hard drives go to them. Step 4 (the budgets) has **Generators you already have**, which each phase counts among the generators it runs (see step 4 above).

Your ticks keep this current. When the steps you tick show more than the profile's plan counts, the build plan of the phase you are working on says so in one notice, in its **Overview** tab: a better miner or belt (you ticked **Advanced Steel Production** for Miner Mk.2, **Leading-Edge Production** for Mk.3, or a **Logistics** milestone for Mk.3 to Mk.6 belts, and your phase would otherwise plan with less), an alternate recipe whose unlock step you ticked but the plan does not use yet, or generators you marked running beyond what a phase from yours on runs. A milestone your phase unlocks anyway is not counted. **Recalculate in place with what you have** recalculates the profile with those choices added, after asking, as **Edit settings** does: your progress is carried and the version before is kept as a backup profile. Nothing changes until you press it. **Dismiss** hides the notice in this browser until your ticks show something new. **Edit settings** also starts **What you already have** from your ticks (the miners and belts and generators on step 4, the alternates on step 2), says which ones it filled in, and Review lists them under **What changes**; nothing is recalculated until you press **Recalculate in place**.

A profile plans production from the phase it was created for onward: if you start at Phase 3, its Review table and its expansion tables begin at Phase 3, because you have already passed Phases 1 and 2 and each phase plan is self-contained. Its phase picker still offers Phases 1 and 2, as milestone-only phases: each lists only the HUB milestones and MAM research that belong to it, with no production lines, storage or power, and its build plan, **Factories**, **Logistics**, **Storage room** and **Power & resources** pages say so and offer **Go to Phase 3**.

The phase picker at the top (the row of phases, or the drop-down on a phone) only shows a phase: look at a later phase or back at an earlier one, and the phase you work on stays the same, in this tab and everywhere else. While it shows another phase it reads **Showing** instead of **Working on**, the phase you work on keeps a dashed outline, the build plan says "You are working on Phase 3" with **Go to Phase 3**, and **Work on Phase 4** beside the picker makes the phase shown the one you work on. With the keyboard, the arrow keys move along the phases and Enter or Space shows the one you are on. When you open a profile while an earlier phase still has an open step, it opens on that phase, with the picker reading **Showing**; a reload or a new tab opens the phase you work on. Where a phase stops using a line an earlier phase built, the build plan ends with a step naming those lines — its budgets no longer count them, and a replacement is usually a different machine rather than an upgrade in place, so commission the replacement before dismantling anything.

The wizard supports Phases 1–5. Post-game retains Phase 5 capacity and directs surplus to storage and sinks. Its optional storage template includes 132 addresses, collectables bays Q/R and the workshop underneath. New saves have no pre-completed steps.

The build plan opens on **Build sequence**: the steps, with the Space Elevator delivery beside them. **Overview** has the phase's figures (production lines running, storage verified, power and delivery time, each a link to its page) and its notices; a number on the tab counts the notices that ask for something, such as a recalculation, power still short or the next phase, and goes once you act on them or dismiss them. **Hard drives** has Best next hard drive and **Somersloops** where a somersloop pays off most. A tab with nothing to show for the phase is left out. Arrow keys move between the tabs.

When you tick a step on the build plan, or press **Mark done**, it is saved at once but stays where it is for a few seconds before it moves into **Done**, so the next step never slides under your pointer. A short message says what you finished, how many steps of the phase are done and which step is next, with **Undo** (or Ctrl+Z, ⌘Z on a Mac) to untick it again. Undo only ever unticks the step in the profile you ticked it in: opening another profile or signing out takes the message and its Undo away.

## Storage room and notes

On the storage map, **Complete room** marks all four checklist steps for the room's selected containers. Each container also has a direct **Done** checkbox; click its item icon/name for individual checks and notes. Unchecking Done clears that container's four steps. Notes save themselves as you type, everywhere in the planner — the line under each notes box says when it was saved, or offers Retry if a save failed; emptying a note deletes it. If someone changes the same note in another tab or on another device while you are typing in it, nothing is overwritten: the other version appears under your note, and you choose **Keep mine**, **Keep theirs** or **Keep both** (theirs first, yours under it). Until you choose, your text stays in the box unsaved, and leaving the page asks first. Existing container addresses and progress keys are unchanged.

**Notes** keeps the save-wide notes and every phase's notes on one page: the save-wide notes first, then the phase you are working on, open, and the other phases folded underneath. The build plan links there with **Phase notes →**.

## Make the plan your own

- **Build plan → Edit steps** lets you rearrange steps with the arrow buttons, rewrite a step's title and details, link a step to one of your production lines, or remove steps you do not want. Removed steps keep their checkmarks and can be restored from **Removed steps** while editing; clearing an edited field restores the original text. Calculated production steps link to their production line automatically: **Production line ↗** opens its details, and **Open factory:** _name_ **→** opens the **Build order →** page of the factory the build plan builds it with, at that line. A line split over several factories says so under the two links; a line in no factory has only **Production line ↗**.
- **Factories → Edit factories** sorts production into named factories — the physical sites of your world. A production line can join several factories with a production split: for example Wire at 300/min in your cable works and the rest beside stitched plates. Leave a rate empty for the whole output or the rest. Removing a factory never touches its production lines or their progress. A factory can also make parts for itself; see **Made on site** below. Once you have factories, the build plan lists each phase's production steps factory by factory, so you can finish one site before travelling to the next: a factory that feeds another comes first, and a factory is interrupted only when it needs something another factory makes. A production line split between factories is listed with the factory that has the largest share of it, and lines in no factory come after the factories. Only the order changes — every step keeps its checkmark and notes — and a phase whose steps you rearranged yourself keeps your order.
- **Made on site.** A part such as Wire is normally made on one central line and carried to every factory that uses it. A factory can make it for itself instead, next to the lines that use it. While editing factories, **Made on site in** _factory_ lists the parts that a line of the plan makes and that the factory's lines use, in every phase the plan builds, not only the one on screen. The factory's own lines made on site don't count here, because a recalculation sizes them to the factory's other lines. The parts used in the phase on screen come first. Then, as soon as you tick a part, come its ingredients that the plan makes, each marked with the part, for example _(for Wire made on site)_. Parts only used in other phases come last, below a line and a small heading that names those phases, such as **Used only by this factory's lines in Phases 4 and 5**. Each of them names the lines that use it, for example _(used by Stator)_, so you can see why a part that no card on the page uses is offered. Parts used in different sets of phases get a heading each. Raw resources such as Water are never offered, and neither are parts no factory can make for itself, such as Encased Uranium Cell, or Heavy Oil Residue where the plan makes it only as a byproduct. Tick the parts the factory makes for itself and press **Save**. A part the factory marked but no longer uses stays on the list, above those headings, marked _(no line here uses it now)_, so you can clear it. A raw resource, or such a part, marked in an older save stays on the list the same way, marked _(can't be made on site)_, or _(a byproduct of central lines covers it)_ for a part such as Heavy Oil Residue that the phase on screen makes only as a byproduct, and a recalculation leaves it out.

  Outside editing, the factory's heading has two lists for the phase on screen. **Made on site:** names the parts the factory has its own line for in the plan. A part there that the factory no longer marks reads _(until a recalculation)_. **Marked, not made on site:** names the parts the factory marks that have no such line, and says why, for example _(needs a recalculation)_ right after you save a new mark.

  Saving does not change the plan. **Save** looks unavailable until you tick or clear a box, and then the picker says _Not saved yet._ After you save, it says **Saved.** While the plan needs a recalculation it adds **This plan now needs a recalculation.** and offers **Go to the recalculation ↑**. That button scrolls to the notice at the top of the Factories page and puts the focus on its button. The notice says **This plan needs a recalculation.**, why, and which factories would make what on site after a recalculation against what this plan makes. The reason is either that the parts your factories mark changed, or that a factory's lines changed: for example _Alpha marks Wire, but none of its lines uses it now; a recalculation would drop Alpha's Wire line._ A part a factory marks but none of its lines uses never asks for a recalculation, since it would get no line. The notice offers **Recalculate in place with items made on site**. Nothing is recalculated until you press it and confirm. It recalculates this profile in place, as **Edit settings** does: the profile keeps its name and your progress, and the version before is kept whole as a separate profile, _<name> (before edit, Oct 7, 2:05 PM)_, under **Saves & profiles**.

  In the new plan, each factory that marks a part has its own line for it, sized to what that factory's own lines use. A central line still makes what everything else needs. A factory gets no line in a phase where none of its lines uses the part. A factory's own line is always one whose main product is the part: a line that makes the part only as a byproduct, such as Rocket Fuel with its Compacted Coal, stays central. When the plan makes a part only that way, such as Heavy Oil Residue from Plastic and Rubber, the factory gets no line for it, and its heading lists the part with _(a byproduct of central lines covers it)_. When central lines make a fluid the factory marks as a byproduct, such as Dark Matter Residue from the Space Elevator parts, that byproduct is piped to the factory first, because a fluid can't go to the AWESOME Sink, and the factory's own line makes only the rest. **Logistics → Between factories** shows the byproduct going into the factory, and **Profile assumptions** says so. If the byproduct covers all the factory uses, the factory gets no line for it, its heading lists the part with _(a byproduct of central lines covers it)_ as well, and no recalculation is asked for. A factory can also make an ingredient on site, such as Copper Ingot for its Wire, and then its own Copper Ingot line feeds its own Wire line. Tick Wire and Copper Ingot together, save, and one recalculation plans both lines. **Profile assumptions** on the build plan names the factories and what they make. The lines keep their size until you recalculate again: moving production lines between factories afterwards does not resize them.

  Say a factory named Alpha makes Wire on site. Its own Wire line is a production line of its own, with its own tick. Its card sits in Alpha and says **Made on site for Alpha**. In the build plan and in **Built so far** its step is **Wire for Alpha**, listed with Alpha's steps. On Alpha's **Build order →** page it is a card of its own, **Wire for Alpha ↗**, whose details open under the same name, that feeds only Alpha's lines. On **Logistics → Between factories** no Wire goes into Alpha, and none goes between two factories that both make it. What the line makes beyond Alpha's needs goes to the AWESOME Sink. When that leftover could meet what a central Wire line would be built for, and that central line would send almost all it makes to the sink, the plan leaves the central line out and Alpha's leftover goes there instead: **Logistics → Between factories** then shows that part leaving Alpha. Anything else the line makes beside the part, such as the Water an Aluminum Scrap line makes, goes to the rest of the plan like any other line's, even when the factory marked it after you recalculated. Water always does, because it is a raw resource and never made on site. A part the factory had already marked when you recalculated, such as Heavy Oil Residue beside its Plastic, feeds the factory's own lines first, as long as the plan makes that part on site for the factory. These pages follow the plan as it was calculated: ticking or clearing a mark changes nothing there until you recalculate.

  If the factories' whole-machine lines need more than your budgets allow in a phase, while central lines fit, that phase makes those parts centrally instead. **Profile assumptions** and ADA say which parts and which factories, and the factory's heading lists such a part with _(made centrally in this phase)_. Raise a budget a little, or mark fewer parts.

  **Ticks on the old lines.** The recalculation carries production line ticks the usual way: a line stays ticked where the new plan needs no more machines and no more input, keeps its built machines where it needs more (its step says how many to add), and is unticked for review otherwise. Two kinds of tick have no single line to land on, so they are kept for review rather than moved or dropped. One is a ticked central line in a phase that gains a factory's own line for the same recipe: some of its machines may now belong to a factory. The other is a ticked line of a factory's own that the new plan no longer has, because the factory stopped marking the part or was removed. Both are listed under **Ticks kept for review** at the foot of **Notes**, each with where its machines are now, for example _Now made on site for Alpha, and on a central line._ A central line on that list that the new plan still has starts unticked, and a factory's new line starts unticked like any new line. Tick the lines that stand in your world. Nothing on the list is deleted, and a profile made from this one later takes it along. The message after the recalculation says how many production lines are left unticked for review, if any, and the name the version before is kept under.
- **Factories → Mark all running** in a factory's header ticks **Running** on every production line of that factory in the phase shown, in one go, also the lines the search or the status chips hide. Lines already marked running stay as they are, lines of other factories and lines in no factory are not touched, and a line split over several factories is the same line in each. Nothing is recalculated. A message says how many lines changed, with **Undo** (or Ctrl+Z, ⌘Z on a Mac), which puts each of those lines back as it was. Once every line of the factory runs, the button reads **Mark all not running** and clears them the same way.
- **Factories → Build order →** on a factory with more than one production line opens that factory's own page: its production lines as numbered cards in the order to build them, suppliers before the lines they feed, with a coloured lane from each output to every line it feeds inside the factory, and what comes in from outside and leaves for other factories, the AWESOME Sink or storage. Hover or focus a card to light its lanes; **The diagram as a table** says the same in words, and **Print** prints the page. A line's **↗** opens that line's details, and closing them brings you back to the same place. The page has its own address, which names the phase it shows, so a bookmark, a reload or the link opened in a new tab shows the same phase's build order; **← Factories** goes back to the Factories page in that phase. It replaces the earlier Build order dialog.
- **Factories → Flow graph ↗** on any factory with a production line opens its flow graph over the Factories page, for the phase shown: what comes in on the left (raw resources, existing supply, other factories), the factory's production lines from left to right in the order they feed each other, and what leaves on the right (other factories, the Space Elevator, protected storage, the AWESOME Sink). Each arrow is one item with its rate per minute, the same figures as the **Build order →** page, the production lines' details and **Logistics**; a dashed arrow under the boxes is a recycling loop, and a line that takes back some of its own output says so in its box. The graph takes the whole width of the window and most of its height (on a phone it keeps a smaller box). The drawing scrolls inside its own box with its scrollbars or, once it has focus, the arrow keys; press on its background and drag to move it (one finger on a touch screen). The mouse wheel over the drawing zooms in and out around the pointer, two fingers pinch to zoom, and **−**, **+** and **Fit** zoom from the keyboard or a touchpad, **Fit** to the whole graph; outside the drawing the wheel scrolls as usual. A press on a production line's box, without dragging, opens that production line in the graph's place. Drag a production line's box, or the box of an input or an output (what comes in on the left; the factories, protected storage and the AWESOME Sink on the right), to move it, and its arrows follow. Everything you move snaps to a 5 px grid, so boxes line up easily. With the keyboard, Tab to a box (they come in the graph's own order, wherever you put them), move it with the arrow keys, 5 px at a time (Shift for 50 px), and open a production line with Enter. The graph remembers each factory's layout, zoom and scroll position, in both editions and in backups, exports and shares, and opens the same way next time, also after a reload. When the plan changes, the lines, inputs and outputs you placed keep their spots, a new line starts near the lines it is linked to, and a line the plan no longer has drops its spot. **Reset layout** beside **Fit** puts the automatic layout back at 100%, inputs and outputs included (it asks first when you moved a box). Arrows into protected storage and the AWESOME Sink are thin grey lines behind the others, without a label or arrowhead; point at one to light it up with its item and rate. Point at a box or Tab to it (every box takes focus) to light up all its arrows and fade the rest, each with its item and rate: on a production line its arrows to storage and the sink too, and on protected storage or the AWESOME Sink every arrow in. Labels slide along their arrows so none overlap; one with no room left is left out (at rest until you point at its arrow), and its value is still in the arrow's hover title and the table. **Show storage and sink as tags** beside **Reset layout** hides those two boxes and their arrows and puts small tags on each production line's right edge instead, "▣ 2/min" for storage and "♻ 4.3/min" for the sink, each naming its items when you point at it or Tab to it; the graph remembers the choice per factory with its layout (**Reset layout** keeps it), and protected storage and the AWESOME Sink keep the spot you moved them to for when you turn the tags off again. **The graph as a table** lists every arrow in words, storage and sink flows included. The graph never changes the plan: moving a box is only how it is drawn. Escape or **×** closes it.
- **Factories → Export to Satisfactory Tools** beside **Flow graph ↗** saves that factory's production lines in the phase shown as a `.sft` file for the production planner on [satisfactorytools.com](https://satisfactorytools.com) (**Production planner → Import**), to check or tweak them there. It asks for what the factory sends elsewhere per minute (other factories, the Space Elevator, protected storage, fuel, and the fuel of its own generators; not what goes to the AWESOME Sink), takes what comes in from other factories and existing supply as inputs, allows the alternate recipes its lines use, and leaves mined raw resources to that planner's miners within the phase's budgets. The file is made in your browser: nothing is sent anywhere or saved.
- **Byproducts.** Many lines make something besides their main product, such as the Water an Aluminum Scrap line makes or the Silica from Alumina Solution. A production line's details say where each byproduct goes, under **Byproducts**, with a link to every line named: for example _Send all of it back to Alumina Solution ↗, which feeds this line._ A line that uses the byproduct of others says where that input comes from: _116.27 m³ recycled from Aluminum Scrap ↗, 7.5 m³ recycled from Battery ↗ in Industrial parts; extract the other 258.04 m³._ The build plan's step for the line says the same after its outputs, unless you rewrote the step, and a factory's **Build order →** page repeats it under the row it concerns, marked ♻, and again in **The diagram as a table**. For a production line split over several factories, that page gives only the factory's own part: what that part sends and takes, at its rates.

  The advice follows one rule, the same one **Logistics → Between factories** and every **Build order →** page use. A line that uses its own byproduct takes it back first. Then the lines of its own factory use it. What a factory has left goes to the factory that is short of the most, then the next, so an item never goes both ways between two factories. Whatever is still left is stored or sunk: a solid reads _store or sink the other 2.05_, or _No line uses it: store it or send it to the AWESOME Sink._ when no line uses it. A fluid never goes to the sink: the plan balances fluids exactly. Mining, extraction and production you already run cover what is still missing. A line followed by _in_ and a factory's name is in another factory, and _back to …, which feeds this line_ marks a recycling loop, which needs a starter batch. In a line's details, the AWESOME Sink row says why the output goes there: _whole-machine rounding surplus_ only when rounding up to whole machines explains it, _left over from using up Heavy Oil Residue_ for a line such as Petroleum Coke that the plan runs to use up a fluid byproduct, _made alongside …, which this phase uses_ for a line that runs for another of its products, or _more than this phase uses_.

  **Pools.** When several lines of a factory make a fluid and several use it, such as Heavy Oil Residue from Plastic and Rubber going to Petroleum Coke and Residual Fuel, their pipes join, so every line in it gives that pool once rather than a share of each line's fluid to each user: _Pool it with the others: 162 m³ in from Rubber ↗ and this line; out 129 m³ to Alternate: Diluted Fuel ↗ (≈ 4/5), 33 m³ to Petroleum Coke ↗ (≈ 1/5) and < 1 m³ to Alternate: Coated Cable ↗ in Copper & caterium (< 1/16)._ A line using it reads _From one pool: …_. The amounts are rounded to the pool's size (whole m³ from 100 m³ up) so that the parts add up to the total, and each part has its share of the pool as a simple fraction.

  **Water Extractors.** Every line that takes Water says how many Water Extractors to build for the Water no byproduct covers (_No byproduct covers it: extract all of it._ when none does), in two ways: at 100%, and overclocked up to 250% with three Power Shards in each full extractor. You choose; the plan only suggests one and names it first, the one its power counts. With budgets per phase that is 100% unless **Suggest overclocked Water Extractors** is ticked (and you can overclock by then); without them, your survey's clock (250% by default). For example: _Water Extractors at 100% (suggested): 2 at 100% + 1 at 15.03% (3 extractors, 41.63 MW). Or 1 overclocked at 215.03% (1 extractor, 3 Power Shards, 55.03 MW)._ Where the plan counts on no Power Shards yet (a Phase 2 or 3 calculated at 100% before plans tapped their nodes overclocked), the overclocked way still gives its count, after researching Power Shards in the MAM: _Or overclocked (after researching Power Shards in the MAM: Blue Power Slugs, then Overclock Production): 13 at 250% + 1 at 132.06% (14 extractors, 40 Power Shards, 901.9 MW)._ Phase 1, before the MAM, gives only the 100% way. With budgets per phase the build plan's **Tap the resource nodes** table has a Water row for the phase's Water in all, and **Power available now** says which way the phase's power counts. Each clock is the one you set in the game. The last extractor runs slower so that the clocks add up to exactly the Water needed, because a pipe cannot overflow: extractors that make more than the line takes fill the pipe and keep stopping. That last clock is rounded down to the hundredth of a percent shown, so the extractors never make more than the line needs, and at most 0.012 m³/min less. When one extractor at 100% or less is enough, both ways are the same, so only one is named. The MW help you plan your grid; the plan's power figures count the phase's extraction as a whole, at your survey's clock (see Calculation scope below).
- **Logistics → Between factories** gives each link one way to travel, and every factory's **Build order →** page names the same: belts and pipes of the best mark you have, a vehicle you picked for the link (_by freight train_ on the factory's page), or, for Water, _Water Extractors on site_: Water Extractors work on any body of water, so you build them at the factory that uses the Water, with no pipe or vehicle between places, and each production line's details say how many. Small items on one link share a mixed belt instead of a belt each: the items under a quarter of a belt (120/min on Mk.4 belts) ride _1 × Mk.4 mixed belt_, and Smart Splitters sort them where it arrives (the AWESOME Sink takes them mixed). A train's freight car loads and unloads through the two belt ports of its platform, so one car moves up to two belts (and a fluid car two pipes), and the link says how many cars that comes to. A truck, tractor or explorer starts on Packaged Fuel; you can pick packaged Fuel, Turbofuel, Rocket Fuel, Ionized Fuel, Liquid Biofuel, Oil or Heavy Oil Residue, Battery, Coal, Compacted Coal, Petroleum Coke or Solid Biofuel. A link that carries a fluid can also go by **Fluid Truck** from Tier 5 (Phase 3): the fluid travels unpackaged, 3,200 m³ a tank, each fluid in trucks of its own, and the link says how many trucks, how full each trip is, how many tank loads a minute the flow fills and what they burn (as a truck burns). Before Phase 3 the choice says when it comes; packaging the fluid for a truck, tractor or explorer stays available. A Fluid Truck carries only fluids, so a solid item on the same link stays on its belt. When the vehicles you pick burn fuel, the panel says how much and offers **Recalculate in place with transport fuel**, which plans that fuel as extra demand in this profile, carries your progress and keeps the version before as a backup profile.
- **Power & resources → Miners and extractors** (with budgets per phase): the Resources page gives each phase's budgets as its own equipment gets them, and lists for every resource the phase draws the nodes to tap, best first — _Tap 6 pure and 7 normal nodes with Miner Mk.2: 12 at 100% and 1 at 41.67% (195 MW)._ — with the last miner underclocked to the remainder. Crude oil and nitrogen get their extractors at 100% and, wherever the phase overclocks (from Phase 2), also at 250% with three Power Shards each, as Water does, with the last one underclocked so that the pipe never fills. The build plan has the same advice as its **Tap the resource nodes** step, and lists the milestone of every belt, pipe, miner and extractor the phase uses (Tier 7 Logistics Mk.5 in Phase 4), so no link uses a belt you have not unlocked. Logistics names each resource's nodes on its card, and while a belt or pipe mark's milestone is not ticked, says which one and the mark to plan with until then. A profile calculated before this keeps its budgets, numbers and steps until you recalculate it; one calculated while Phases 2 and 3 still planned their miners at 100% keeps them too, and its **Tap the resource nodes** step says how many machines overclocking would save and that **Edit settings** → **Recalculate in place** plans it that way.
- **Machines per delivery.** A production line's details split its machines over the places its output goes, and the counts add up to the line's machines: _split ≈ 7 / 3 / 1 / 1 / <1_ for 12 Constructors, where _<1_ shares a machine with the others. Under each count is the exact part of the line's machines at 100%.
- **Storage room → Edit layout** renames floors and bays, adds your own, and assigns or clears containers. A bay holds the eight printed positions, 01–08, and takes more when you need them: adding a container to a full bay gives it the next address, 09 upwards, marked off below the two banks. Printed addresses never move, and a cleared container keeps its checkmarks for when you assign it again.
- **Build materials.** Every production line's step in the build plan ends with what it takes to build: _To build: 2 Refineries + 1 Water Extractor + Mk.4 belts and Mk.2 pipes_, then the parts those buildings cost, _Materials: 60 × Copper Sheet, 60 × Steel Pipe, …_. It counts only what is still to build: the machines to add on a line that runs since the phase before or that a recalculation grew or switched, none on a line with machines to spare, and only the generators you do not have yet. The Water Extractors are those of the suggested way, counted when the whole line is new. Belts and pipes are named by the best mark you have in that phase, without lengths, since the plan does not know how far apart you build. The building costs are the game's, from the same dataset as the recipes.
- **Today's session** (the link above the build plan's steps, **Today's session: the next builds, printable →**) lists the next 3, 4 or 5 open builds of the phase on screen, in the build plan's order: for each, what it takes to build and its materials, its power, the raw resources it draws, and whether what feeds it already runs (_Ready once build 1 runs._, or _Waiting on lines not marked running: …_). **All together** adds up the materials. **Print** prints the page, and **Copy note** copies a short note for the game's To-Do list, which takes at most 256 characters: the builds with their buildings, then as many parts as fit. How many builds it lists is remembered in this browser, not saved with the profile. Nothing is ticked or recalculated there: tick the steps in the build plan once they run.
- **Saves & profiles → Duplicate** copies a profile including its progress, so you can try changes without touching the original plan.
- **Saves & profiles → Share** downloads a share file with the profile's plan, storage layout, factories, personal tasks and step edits — but none of your checkmarks, notes or delivery counts. Anyone can import it in either edition under **Backup → Import saves**; it arrives as a new save without affecting theirs. (A share link in a URL is not offered: a profile snapshot is far larger than links reliably allow.) Older planner versions refuse a share that contains step edits, factories or container positions past 08 and ask for an update instead of dropping them.
- **Backup → Co-op progress** is for playing one plan together, each on your own copy (for example after a **Share**). **Export ticks** downloads a small file with only the steps you ticked, plus the save and profile name and the time; no plan, settings, notes or delivery counts. **Merge progress from file…** reads such a file (or a progress backup, or a save export with one profile, of which it uses only the ticks) and first shows what merging would do: how many steps it ticks per phase and in the storage room, how many are ticked here already, and the steps this plan does not have, which are skipped and listed. Nothing changes until you press **Merge**. A merge only adds ticks: nothing you ticked is unticked, and the plan is never recalculated. Both editions have it.
- **Factories → owners and Mine only** are for sharing the building work. While you **Edit factories**, each factory has an **Owner** field under its name: type who builds it, such as _Anna_, or leave it empty for nobody. Outside editing, the factory's header says **Owner: Anna**, with _(you)_ when that is your name. The owner belongs to the factory, so it goes along with exports, imports, shares and a new profile carried from this one, and a co-op merge leaves it as it is. Once a factory has an owner, the Factories page and the build plan show **Mine only** and **Your name**. Your name is remembered in this browser only, as **Mine only** is, and both are never saved with the profile; it matches an owner whatever the capitals. With **Mine only** ticked, the Factories page shows only the factories with your name and their production lines, and the build plan only the steps of those lines. Factories without an owner are hidden too, as are other people's factories, the lines in no factory and the steps that are in no factory (milestones, research, deliveries): a factory nobody owns is nobody's yet. While you edit factories every factory shows, so you can set its owner. A profile with an owner needs this version of the planner or later to import it.
- **Ticked by** tells you who ticked a step and when. A ticked step in the build plan or the storage room says, quietly under its title, **Ticked by Anna, 21 Oct** (the day in your language's order, with the year when it is not this year). The name is your **Your name**, which you type under **Backup → Co-op progress** (it is there before any factory has an owner) or in the **Mine only** bar of the Factories page or the build plan, as described above; it is the same name in all three places. With no name set, the step says **Ticked 21 Oct**. Unticking a step removes its note, and ticks made before this version have none. Export ticks and Merge progress from file… carry the notes along, so after a merge your co-op partner's steps say who ticked them, and a progress backup keeps them; a share leaves them out. A profile with these notes needs this version of the planner or later to import it.
- **Recent ticks**, at the end of **Backup → Co-op progress**, lists who ticked what, newest first: for example **Anna ticked Build 4 Constructors (Iron Plate), Oct 21, 2:05 PM · Phase 3** (the date and time in your language's format), or **Ticked …** for a tick made with no name set. It shows the ticks of the open profile's build plan that have such a note, in every phase; ticks from before this kind of note, the storage room's ticks and steps no longer in the plan are not listed. Click a step to open it in the build plan: its phase is shown (not saved as the phase you work on), and the step is shown and marked even while **Hide completed**, **Required steps only** or **Mine only** would hide it, until you leave the build plan. With more than one name in the list, **Show** picks one person's ticks. The first 50 show; **Show more** adds 50 more. The list only reads the notes: nothing is saved or changed.
- **Live refresh (Docker edition).** When two players share one Docker planner, a step one of them ticks shows up on the other's screen within about 12 seconds, without a reload. An open tab checks for changes only while it is visible. While you are typing in a field, editing, or have a dialog open, the page is not redrawn under you: a quiet line at the top says _Progress changed on another device._ with **Refresh**, and the change comes in by itself once you are done. Nothing is recalculated, and **Undo** after a tick only unticks your own step. The browser edition has no server, so it has no live refresh: use **Merge progress from file…** there.

## ADA

ADA — the Artificial Directory and Assistant — sits under the navigation and comments on the plan you have open. Every remark is built from the same counters the pages show: steps left in this phase and which one is next, production lines not yet marked running, unverified container positions, elevator parts short of target, retirement steps still open, resources over the budget you entered, missing power headroom, and (in the browser edition) how long ago you exported a full backup. The tone is a joke; the advice is not, and ADA never invents a number the plan does not already contain.

**Another remark** cycles through everything that applies right now: whole-page states first ("no save is open"), then real problems, then whatever the current page is about, then ADA’s general observations. Keep going past the last one and she notices. **Mute** silences ADA; the choice is remembered in that browser and is never part of a save, so muting changes nothing about your progress and nothing about a transfer.

Prod the small badge beside her name five times in a row and the corporate voice slips into a transmission fault. Prod it again for another one; the last one hands the terminal back, and anything else you click restores normal service immediately. It is decoration — nothing is only reachable that way.

## Progression guidance

Calculated profiles include a Phase 1 construction-stock base, manually supplied Biomass/Solid Biofuel startup, phase-appropriate power commissioning, HUB milestone costs and MAM research suggestions. Mark unlocks in the checklist to update current-power advice; Coal Power is listed from Phase 2 and Petroleum Power from Phase 3 even when the plan builds none of their generators, so you can mark what you already run. With spare existing power in your settings, "Power available now" starts from that figure instead, together with what Phase 5's Alien Power Augmenters add: how much more the phase needs, which new generators the plan builds for it and how much they leave spare, or that your spare power and augmenters cover the phase. Unlock and alternate-recipe checkmarks persist across phases within the profile. Hard-drive tasks list the actual selected alternates and their recorded prerequisites; random scans do not guarantee a fixed drive count. Each alternate is listed once, in the first phase that uses it, so a later phase that uses it again counts it there and not twice. The same goes for the start-up steps you do once in the game: the Biomass and Solid Biofuel steps are listed only in the phase your profile starts in, and "Unlock Coal Power" only in the first phase from Phase 2 on. Each phase that still runs on biomass keeps its own burner bank step, sized for that phase. The step also totals the fuel the bank takes at full load, as a table with one row for each fuel you can burn (Leaves, Wood, Mycelia, Biomass or Solid Biofuel): how much per minute, how much for the phase's planned run time, and how many stacks that is. Burn one kind, not all of them; the totals are an estimate for gathering and are not saved or part of the plan's calculation.

Milestone cost notes use production lines explicitly marked running in this or earlier phases. They do not assume that inventory or spare output exists. Starter construction and biomass lines are separate startup guidance, not added to the frozen continuous-production calculation. Existing production lines and progress IDs are preserved.

## Whole-machine production

New profiles default to running solid-part production machines at 100%. The solver selects a recipe network first and fits whole-machine production within the resource budgets, recalculating upstream requirements and handling liquid byproducts. Extra solids are listed as surplus: supply downstream lines, refill storage, then sink the rest. This can increase the scale of the entire chain, not only the last machine in one production line: every machine runs at full speed, so the lines feeding it do too. On a default plan Phase 5 takes nearly three times the buildings and the power of exact clocks.

The build plan's side column shows **What whole machines cost** in the phase on screen: buildings, power needed, what goes to storage or the sink and the delivery time, each beside the same phase planned with exact clocks, and the raw resources whole machines draw on top. The wizard's Review shows the same for every phase, and the Goals step under the whole-machine box. A profile calculated before this was recorded shows none until you recalculate it.

To run one production line at exact clocks, open it (**Production line ↗**) and tick **Exact clocks for this line**: its last machine is then underclocked to the exact remainder instead of sending the overflow to storage or the sink, and the lines feeding it shrink with it. That removes only what rounding up to whole machines adds: for a line such as Petroleum Coke, which the plan runs to use up Heavy Oil Residue, the note under the box says exact clocks would not remove what goes to the sink. The choice is saved with the profile's progress and changes nothing by itself: the line's build-plan step and card say _Exact clocks after a recalculation_, and the build plan and **Factories** offer **Recalculate in place with exact clocks**, which recalculates the open profile in place with your progress carried over and keeps the version before as a backup profile, as **Edit settings** does. Any other recalculation you start plans the lines you chose too: **Edit settings** starts from them, with Review naming the change (“Exact clocks”), and so do the other recalculation offers. A choice made while an edit was already open stays saved after it, and the build plan offers it again. Untick the box to take the choice back. Fluid lines always run at exact clocks, because a pipe cannot overflow, also when they make a solid beside the fluid, such as Rocket Fuel with its Compacted Coal or Fuel with its Polymer Resin; so do generator and nuclear lines. A profile calculated before Rocket Fuel and Fuel lines kept exact clocks still runs them on whole machines, and offers the box for them, until you recalculate it.

Space Elevator parts are the exception: everything a part's lines make beyond what other lines use goes to the elevator, never to the sink, and the delivery time follows from it. Two Assemblers making 10 Versatile Framework/min deliver all 10, so Phase 3's 2,500 take about 4 h 10 min, not the 7 h 52 min the balanced goal's 5.3/min would take. A profile calculated before this change keeps its own rates until you recalculate it.

A line that only makes a Space Elevator part is a batch task: its step and its production line dialog say how many to make, how long that takes at the planned rate and the power you free by switching the line off afterwards, such as _Batch: Make 100 × Adaptive Control Unit (about 1 h 40 min at this rate), then switch the line off until Phase 4 needs it again: frees 55 MW._ A line that also feeds another line, makes power fuel or fills storage keeps running and has no batch task. The figures come from the plan as it was calculated; nothing is ticked or recalculated.

Once a delivery counter on the build plan reaches its target, the steps of the lines that only made that part are dimmed and moved below your other open steps, with a note under the title: _Smart Plating is delivered: this line is now only needed for Phase 2._, or _no longer needed_ when no later phase builds the line. Nothing is ticked for you. A line whose part another line still uses, such as Nuclear Pasta for the Singularity Cells, stays as it is. The build plan's summary then gives the time the rest of the delivery takes, and **Logistics** marks the part _delivered_ on its link to the Space Elevator.

Each phase of the build plan starts with the power you already run, then the milestones the phase needs, then the power steps that need those unlocks. A phase's power plant comes before the production lines it powers: coal generators first, Fuel Generators straight after the lines that make their fuel. Phase 1 has a **Build the Space Elevator** step before its production lines, and every phase ends its production with **Send the Phase N delivery**, which shows as done once every delivery counter reaches its target. Both show as done once you work in a later phase, since moving on means that delivery was sent. MAM research that only a later phase needs is listed last, marked _(optional)_, with the phase that needs it; tick **Required steps only** beside **Hide completed** to hide it and the storage-only lines; when only optional steps are left, the list says so. Like **Hide completed**, that choice is remembered in this browser only. The hard-drive step shows as done once every recipe it lists is unlocked. A step done this way says why under its title and is not ticked for you, so your checkmarks stay exactly as you left them.

When every part of the phase is delivered, the build plan says so and offers the next phase (**Go to Phase 3**), which makes it the phase you work on, as **Work on Phase 3** does. From the second phase of a profile on, the build plan opens with a handover from the phase before: the production lines marked running there that this phase builds again are kept, with the machines to add and the clocks to change, then the other lines to build and the lines to retire. A kept line's step starts with what already runs, for example _Running since Phase 1: 8 × Smelter, the last at 7.75%. Add 6 machines, and raise the last Phase 1 Smelter from 7.75% to 100%._ Until you tick them in the new phase, those lines are counted apart at their earlier size: the summary at the top says _0 of 22 production lines running_ and then _13 more lines running since Phase 1_, **Built so far** counts what they already deliver, a production line card on the Factories page says _Running since Phase 1: 8 of 14 machines_, and the production line dialog marks the phases it was marked running in. On the Factories page each factory, and the lines in no factory, has its own **Handover** line under its heading, the same count over that factory's lines (_From Phase 1: keep 3 lines already running, adding 6 machines and changing 2 clocks; build the other 2; retire 1._); open it for the lines by what this phase does with them: **Keep as is**, **Add or change** (with the step's own words, such as _Add 6 machines, …_), **New in Phase 2**, **Not marked running in Phase 1** and **Retire** (the retire step's lines, in the factory they were in). It goes once the factory's lines are all marked running here. Nothing is ticked for you: tick the line Running in the new phase once it matches, or press **Mark all running** on a factory once all of its lines do. After Phase 5 the button goes to Post Phase 5, whose finish card says it is not a sixth phase: it keeps the Phase 5 lines running and has no delivery of its own.

Fluid, generator and nuclear/waste-processing lines remain precisely balanced and may retain underclocks. Detailed instructions separate total machines, full-speed machines and the adjustable machine, and show output per machine next to its clock setting.

For a previously calculated profile, use **Factories → Round up production**. This creates a new profile revision, copies notes/unlocks/progress, and clears completed production line checks only where increased inputs or machine counts need review. The previous profile and all its progress remain untouched.

If resource limits or solver limits prevent a rounded plan, affected phases are flagged for review. When only the whole-machine fit exceeds a budget, the draft names each short resource with the least rate that would fit and offers the alternative of precise balancing. Under the reason, **What runs out** sets each short resource against what the map gives at the nodes you allow (your node survey less the nodes it leaves out, or the world's known nodes; never an estimate) and says how much of it is fuel for power; **Options** then lists only the levers that apply to that phase: a budget the map can still give, more time (the least hours per phase it fits in, with **Recalculate in place at … h per phase** under a target completion time), alternates that use less of the short resource, Production Amplifiers, SAM conversion in Phase 5, nuclear power from Phase 4 and, for a phase that fits until its build costs are counted, to stop counting them (Game settings). Each option's button opens **Edit settings** on the step that holds it; nothing is recalculated until you confirm. Whole-machine maximum output is bounded to the selected recipe network; it is not a global mixed-recipe integer optimum.

## Calculation scope

The generated plans solve material and resource constraints using HiGHS. Standard recipes are always available at their modeled phase; the alternate option enables phase-eligible alternates. Pure ingots override other ingot recipes when available. Actual recipe/milestone unlocking still has to happen in game.

Fixed-time plans minimize production-building equivalents (or, with another **What the plan keeps lowest**, the power or raw resources they draw, or your weighted mix), then report whole buildings and the last machine’s underclock. They do not claim the absolute minimum number of integer buildings. Minimal construction then runs those buildings as fast as they allow: each phase is solved again for the fastest delivery with no line given more machines than the 24-hour plan builds, and keeps that plan when it finishes sooner (Review shows the 24-hour time beside it, and **Profile assumptions** names the phases). Phase 1, which runs on hand-fed biomass, keeps its 24-hour plan. Maximum output maximizes simultaneous elevator delivery within the entered budgets and selected recipe set, then minimizes building equivalents at that output. It does not optimize AWESOME Sink points.

SAM policies affect raw-resource conversion only. Essential SAM ingredients remain available. Full nuclear recycling burns plutonium and Ficsonium rods in Phase 5, enforcing zero accumulated radioactive waste. Phase 4 sinks plutonium fuel rods until Ficsonium is available.

Alien Power Augmenters are Phase 5 buildings. Available power is `(new generators + existing installed + 500 per augmenter) x (1 + 0.1 per unfueled + 0.3 per fueled)`, less the existing consumption implied by your spare figure. Profiles with no augmenters calculate exactly as before. Somersloop amplification of production machines is not modelled.

Items the AWESOME Sink cannot accept — Power Shards among them — are balanced exactly rather than run whole at 100%: they have nowhere to overflow, so a rounded-up line would back up and stall. Their last machine is underclocked, and they are never listed as sinkable surplus.

Production amplification is fitted to the recipe network the unamplified solve chooses, and only the largest lines are offered a somersloop, so the result is not a global optimum over amplified and unamplified recipes together. Amplified machines are planned as whole machines at 100%. If the search for a phase's amplified plan stops before it can prove the best plan, the phase is planned without amplification and says so, rather than losing a plan that fits.

New power plants and their fuel inputs are included. Coal Generators burn Coal, Compacted Coal or Petroleum Coke, and Fuel Generators burn Fuel, Turbofuel, Rocket Fuel (from Phase 4) or Ionized Fuel (in Phase 5). The main power choice in the settings narrows them: **Coal** keeps every Coal Generator fuel, so the Petroleum Coke a plan makes from Heavy Oil Residue is burnt instead of sent to the AWESOME Sink, and **Fuel** keeps Fuel and Ionized Fuel. Under any choice but **Coal** and **Let the planner choose**, the plan sinks spare Petroleum Coke and Compacted Coal, because burning them in Coal Generators would need more machines than they save: the step and the production line dialog of the line making it say so, and point at coal power, which burns it. A plan calculated before keeps its generators until you recalculate it. Existing power is entered as **spare** capacity; its resource use must already be deducted from the entered budgets. The plan sizes its own generators from one power model, which the Resources page's power bar, the notice above the build plan, the build plan's summary and its "Power available now" step, and Review's **Power needed** column all show, so they always agree. What a phase needs is its production lines at their real clocked power (an underclocked machine draws clock^1.32 of its power, not its full power), the utility allowance on those lines (20% by default, for trains, drone ports and pumps), and the **miners and extractors** for the raw resources it draws (your node survey's miner and clock, else Miner Mk.3 at 250%, on normal nodes; Oil Extractors, Water Extractors and resource-well pressurizers at the same clock; with budgets per phase, the phase's own miner on the nodes its draw taps, best first, with the last one slower). Particle Accelerators, Converters and Quantum Encoders, whose power rises and falls with each production cycle, are counted at their peak, and the Resources page also gives their average; every other building at its fixed power, as in the game. What a phase has is **whole generators at 100%** (generators burn fuel only for the power drawn, so the last one is never underclocked), plus Phase 5's augmenters and your spare power. A phase keeps the generators the phase before built where it still runs that kind of generator, so they are not planned again or retired. The generators you already have (step 4) count the same way from your start phase on; they are not spare power, because the plan still plans their fuel. From Phase 2 on a plan that fits therefore always has the power it needs. Its fuel lines make what that need burns: generators burn fuel only for the power drawn, so fuel made beyond it would back up the fuel lines and, through their Heavy Oil Residue, the Plastic and Rubber lines. The plan makes at most one generator's worth more; where whole lines make more Heavy Oil Residue than the fuel needs, the rest goes another way, such as Petroleum Coke for the sink. It can have more power, when whole generators round up or when a kept generator is not needed, and "Power available now" says which. A profile calculated before this keeps its fuel lines, and may say its lines make more fuel than the phase needs, until you recalculate it. Phase 1 requires biomass or existing power. A profile calculated before this power model keeps the figures it was calculated with until you recalculate it. This is a steady-state estimate, not a simulation of startup, variable demand or your actual game grid.

Storage is an explicit protected output per item. In a whole-machine plan the wizard or **Round up production** calculates, it takes the plan's surplus of each item first, with no extra machine, and a full container overflows to the AWESOME Sink (in game, a Priority Merger or an overflow splitter after it). Only an item with no surplus gets **storage-only lines**: they run at exact clocks, usually one underclocked machine, are titled "… for storage (optional)" and come after the storage step at the end of each phase's build plan, since nothing else uses them. Their production line dialog lists only where their own output goes, at their own rates: protected storage, the other storage-only lines they feed on the way there, and the generators that burn more fuel to power them; the plan's other lines making the item feed everything else. An item with a per-item rate of its own is topped up to that rate the same way; any other item with some surplus takes only that surplus. The Resources page says where each container's rate comes from, and Review shows how many buildings the storage-only lines add. A plan calculated before this keeps its storage lines and numbers until you recalculate. Unpackaged fluids, radioactive items and gathered feedstock are excluded from continuous storage production. Gathered items still have storage locations. Some standard-recipe items need alternates to become sustainably automatable. Singularity Cells are an explicit extra supply rate; teleport use and operating power are not inferred automatically.

Purity does not determine randomized node counts. Reference budgets use standard counts at endgame extraction; oil wells are excluded and nitrogen is separate. Edit the budgets to match your seed and accessible mining. Other mods/settings are notes only: changed recipes, boosts and modded items are not simulated. The app does not read game-save files.

## Your data in the browser edition

The browser edition's calculator runs in a browser worker and saves profiles, checkmarks, notes and delivery counts in IndexedDB on that browser. No account is required and save contents are not uploaded to GitHub. GitHub serves the site and can receive ordinary web access information.

Browser data is specific to the browser, device and site address. Clearing site data deletes saves; private browsing may discard them when closed. Use **Backup → Export all saves** regularly. Import adds separate copies rather than overwriting existing saves. The **Request persistent browser storage** button on the Backup page asks for protection from automatic eviction where supported; it is not a backup.

## New to Satisfactory

Tick **New to Satisfactory** on the guided start’s first screen, or above the build plan’s steps, to keep the planner simple while you learn the game. The guided start then skips the questions on alternate recipes and clock speeds while their answers are the simple ones (standard recipes, whole machines at 100%), and a new save starts with nothing stocked beyond the Space Elevator, which keeps Phase 1 to a few biomass burners. The build plan hides the hard-drive payoff and what whole machines cost. It is a view setting of this browser: it changes no saved profile and recalculates nothing, and All settings still has every setting. Untick it to see everything again.

## Expert mode

Tick **Expert mode** on the guided start’s first screen, above All settings’ steps, or above the build plan’s steps if you know the game. A new plan then opens straight on All settings, which shows every setting on three denser screens (game settings with preferences, goals with resources, and Review) without the explanatory paragraphs; the field labels and the ⓘ help tips stay, and **← Guided start** still takes you to the questions. The same answers give the same plan either way. On the build plan, the HUB milestones and MAM research are grouped by tier, each tier with its own tick (“Tier 3 done”) beside each unlock’s own: ticking a tier ticks every unlock in it that is not ticked yet, exactly as ticking each one would, and unticking it unticks only that tier’s unlocks. The toast offers Undo. The expert controls that shape the plan itself are ordinary settings, the same in either mode: what the plan keeps lowest and the caps per phase on step 3 (Goals), and the nodes left out of the plan in the node survey. Like New to Satisfactory, it is a view setting of this browser that changes no saved profile and recalculates nothing; the two are never on together, so ticking one unticks the other.

## Glossary

The planner explains these game terms where they first appear on a page: hover over the dotted word, tab to it, or tap it.

- **MAM**: The Molecular Analysis Machine, the research building. You feed it items such as ores, Power Slugs, Somersloops and hard drives to unlock research: Caterium, Quartz, Sulfur, Power Shards and more. _Where to get it:_ Build it after the Tier 1 milestone Field Research in the HUB.
- **SAM**: An alien raw resource. Late-game parts use it, mostly as Reanimated SAM and SAM Fluctuators, and converters turn it into other resources. _Where to get it:_ Mine it from SAM nodes with a Miner, then research it in the MAM to process it.
- **Power Slugs**: Glowing blue, yellow and purple creatures found in the world. A Constructor turns each one into Power Shards: 1, 2 or 5. _Where to get it:_ Pick them up while exploring, often on cliffs and in caves. Research them in the MAM (Power Slugs) to unlock the shard recipes.
- **Power Shards**: They let a machine run faster than 100%. Each shard in a machine raises its limit by 50%, up to 250% with three. _Where to get it:_ Craft them from Power Slugs in a Constructor, after the MAM research Blue Power Slugs and Overclock Production.
- **Hard drives**: Data drives salvaged from crashed drop pods. Analysing one in the MAM offers a choice of alternate recipes, and you keep the one you pick. _Where to get it:_ Open the crash sites around the map. Some need power, an item or a cleared path before they open.
- **AWESOME Sink**: A building that destroys the items you feed it and pays you points for them. The points print coupons for the AWESOME Shop. _Where to get it:_ Unlock the Tier 2 milestone Resource Sink Bonus Program in the HUB.
- **Underclock**: Running a machine slower than 100% by lowering its clock speed. It makes and uses less, and draws less power. _Where to get it:_ Set the clock speed in any machine’s menu. It needs nothing and works from the start.
- **Overclock**: Running a machine faster than 100%, up to 250%. It needs Power Shards in the machine and draws much more power. _Where to get it:_ Put Power Shards in the machine’s menu once the MAM research Overclock Production is done.
- **Somersloops**: Rare alien artifacts. Filling a production machine’s somersloop slots doubles what it makes at four times the power (production amplification): 1 in a Constructor or Smelter, 2 in an Assembler, Foundry, Refinery or Converter, 4 in a Manufacturer, Blender, Particle Accelerator or Quantum Encoder. Building an Alien Power Augmenter costs 10. _Where to get it:_ Find them around the map; the world holds 106. In the MAM’s Alien Technology research, analyse one (Somersloop Analysis), then research Production Amplifier so machines take them.
- **Alternate recipes**: Other recipes for a part, often with cheaper or different inputs. The standard recipe stays available. _Where to get it:_ Analyse a hard drive in the MAM and pick one of the recipes it offers.
